Follow this chronological roadmap during your first 14 days in Girne to ensure a smooth administrative, academic, and social transition.

Join the Official WhatsApp Community

Guidance & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions by Freshmen

Clear answers to the most common questions before and after landing in Ercan.

First, purchase a local SIM card (or use the free airport Wi-Fi) to alert the FIUCC Reception team at +90 548 800 0000. If you registered for the official university airport shuttle beforehand, proceed to the FIU arrival greeting booth near the main exit. If not, inform the community team, and a delegate in Girne will coordinate your bus or taxi directly to campus.
You must apply online through the TRNC Ministry of Interior portal after completing university registration. You will need your passport, Student Certificate (Öğrenci Belgesi), certified rental contract or dorm certificate, and payment for government health blood tests. The FIUCC Welfare Bureau holds weekly in-person lab filing sessions to walk you through it step-by-step.
Final International University is an English-medium institution, but our Congolese community has more than 450 French speakers. In your daily social life, Lingala, Swahili, and French are spoken freely. For your classes, FIUCC arranges weekly bilingual study halls and English tutoring to help you transition seamlessly.
Money transfers are normally completed via Western Union, MoneyGram, Ria, or direct SWIFT transfers to student bank accounts at Ziraat Bank or Garanti Bank once opened. There are multiple certified money exchange agencies right across Girne town center.
There are several dedicated Afro-shops in Girne center and nearby Karakum stocking plantains, fufu flour, pondu, smoked fish, and seasonings. Full-service braiders and barber shops run by senior African students are listed inside the FIUCC Student Community Handbook.
The Solidarity Welfare Fund provides short-term micro-loans, urgent prescription assistance, and food rations for students experiencing unexpected banking delays or illness. Any registered community member can discreetly apply through the Welfare Committee liaison.
Yes. There are thriving bilingual and French-speaking Christian student fellowships meeting every Sunday in Girne, as well as Muslim student prayer halls directly on campus. Transportation carpools are organized weekly via the WhatsApp Community religious boards.
Membership is fully voluntary and open to all Congolese students enrolled at FIU. Standard census registration is 100% free. Voluntary annual community dues (which fund graduation celebrations, cultural galas, and emergency relief funds) can be contributed after orientation.
